# Cambridge 0862 Stage 8 authoring and coverage

The nine units follow the Cambridge Stage 8 Scheme of Work. The course is written for ages 12–13. It uses the local Curriculum Framework for scope, learner book for sequencing and worked-example depth, teacher guide for ways to elicit reasoning, and long-term planning template for grouping objectives. The table maps each framework objective to a named Arena lesson. [Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics 0862](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-lower-secondary/curriculum/mathematics/)

The 61 distinct Stage 8 codes are all mapped. Thinking and Working Mathematically is threaded through tasks that explain a sign or boundary, compare alternative representations, classify a shape, propose a trial, check a hypothesis and improve a survey. The separately generated Stage 8 mathematical-thinking unit extends this strand.

## Teaching references

- The local 0862 Mathematics Curriculum Framework PDF, printed pages 21–26, sets the objectives. The Stage 8 Scheme of Work groups them into its nine units. The Teacher Guide section on Thinking and Working Mathematically informed the choice between a straightforward calculation, a check and a longer explanation. The Long-term Planning Template informed the unit-first order.
- The supplied year-8-lb-4-pdf-free.pdf is Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics Learner’s Book 8, second edition, by Lynn Byrd, Greg Byrd and Chris Pearce. Worked-example and exercise sequences and selected figures were reviewed. Source illustrations, examples and exercise wording were kept separate from authored materials.
- Oak’s Year 8 materials informed the progression from an example to a visual model and a short independent check, especially for linear graphs, percentage change, constructions and data. Its lesson order is different from Cambridge Stage 8. [Year 8 Maths](https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/maths-secondary/units?years=8) · [Linear graphs](https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/maths-secondary-ks3/units/graphical-representations-of-linear-equations/lessons) · [Constructions](https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/maths-secondary-ks3/units/constructions/lessons) · [Statistical representations](https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/programmes/maths-secondary-ks3/units/numerical-summaries-of-data/lessons/comparing-statistical-representations)
- Corbettmaths was consulted for method sequencing and common mistakes in algebraic notation, fractions, angles and bearings. NRICH was consulted for explaining why pair counts produce different chances in a sample space. [Corbettmaths contents](https://corbettmaths.com/contents/) · [NRICH Fair’s Fair](https://nrich.maths.org/problems/fairs-fair)

## Assessment references

The local 2025 Stage 8 Progression Papers 1 and 2 and their mark schemes were used to calibrate reading load, the balance of responses and short method marks. Paper 1 uses no calculator and Paper 2 permits one. Each is marked out of 50. The second local copy of 2025 Paper 1 has the same SHA-256 and was counted once. The older file identified internally as 2023 Stage 8 Paper 1 and its paired support materials supplied additional calibration. The 2026 Live mark schemes were consulted for assessment-support metadata only.

The bank contains independently authored data, wording, contexts, diagrams, solutions and indicative Maths4U marking guidance. Stage 8 uses Progression-style questions for year-level practice. Cambridge Lower Secondary Checkpoint is the end-of-Stage-9 assessment. [Cambridge Progression Tests](https://help.cambridgeinternational.org/hc/en-gb/articles/360000048158-What-are-the-Cambridge-Lower-Secondary-Progression-Tests) · [Cambridge Checkpoint](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-lower-secondary/assessment/cambridge-checkpoint/)

## Stage 8 scheme package totals

The nine units supply 23 lessons, 156 worked examples, 312 in-lesson checks with feedback, 23 A4 revision sheets and 27 SVGs. The bank contains 276 short tasks and 276 fresh short tasks, plus 134 main written tasks and 402 related written variants. The four written instances per family ask the same core mathematical decision using different given data. The totals exclude the separate mathematical-thinking unit.
